Camera move (keyframes)

Intentional camera movement layered on top of the shake: keyframe pan (X/Y) and roll over time. Click the track to seek, drag the diamonds to move keyframes in time. Values ease in and out between keys. Extracting a reference video writes its slow intended movement here as keyframes and keeps only the residual shake as the reference layer.

Export
Frame rate Quality Download

Frame-accurate export: every frame is rendered at its exact timestamp, so no frame is dropped or duplicated, regardless of screen refresh rate or machine load. For the smoothest result, match the frame rate to your source clip (e.g. 60 fps source, 60 fps export). Runs slower than real time. Falls back to real-time recording in browsers without WebCodecs.

Reference shake (adds on top)

Load a reference clip that was actually handheld. The full camera motion is extracted frame by frame: the slow intended movement becomes camera-move keyframes (visible and editable in the camera move timeline), and the residual shake is added to the procedural shake. Set Procedural intensity to 0 for reference-only motion, or Transfer intensity to 0 to keep only the intended movement.

Framing

The shake shifts and rotates the frame, so the source is scaled up to keep edges covered. Auto mode computes the tightest safe zoom over the whole clip.

Settings

Saves all sliders, seed, framing, export options, camera-move keyframes, and the shake extracted from the reference video (if any) as a JSON file. Load it later to restore the exact same motion, including the reference trajectory.

HEVC / H.265 converter

Converts an HEVC phone clip to H.264 MP4 using a WebAssembly build of ffmpeg, entirely in the browser. Downloads ~32 MB once, then the result is loaded as the source video automatically. Audio is converted to AAC. Output is capped at 1080p. Keep the tab open while it runs.
